# Privacy policy inputs

**Purpose.** The privacy policy and the terms will be drafted by Claude just before deployment (owner decision 2026-09-21: written when development is complete, with counsel). This file is the single list of what the product collects, why, how long it is kept, and every rule decided about it, so the draft leaves nothing out and claims nothing the product does not do. **Whoever changes what is collected, stored, shown to a developer, or set in a browser updates this file in the same change.** Last checked against the code: 2026-09-26.

**How to use it.** Draft the policy from sections 1 to 6. Section 7 lists what is decided but not built, section 8 what is still open and needs the owner, section 9 what counsel should be asked. A policy sentence that is not backed by a line here should not be written.

## 3. Analytics (first-party; `DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-25 and 2026-09-26; `docs/schema/schema.v20.md`, `schema.v21.md`)

**What is recorded per event:** the event name (a property viewed; a property added to or removed from a comparison; a comparison opened with the properties in it; a section opened; a unit type switched to; a focus chosen; a comparison shared or saved; a property saved; a dossier unlocked; an enquiry sent with the properties being compared; intake completed with priorities, bedrooms and city; visible time on a dossier or a comparison), whether the visitor was signed in (never who), the property or properties, device class (phone, tablet, desktop), campaign tags and the referring **domain** only, and the coarse budget band of the ceiling stated in intake, from a fixed public list. **Never recorded in analytics:** name, phone, email, account id, IP address, exact budget, price, search text, or any free text.

**Two ways a browser is recorded:**

- **Identified** (the default): a random id in `pc_vid` and a visit id in `pc_sid`. The same id continues after sign-in so a visit's funnel is unbroken; it is **not** linked to an account anywhere.
- **Anonymous**: a browser that sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track. No cookie is set or read, and the event carries no id, so nothing links it to any other event. Crawlers and link previews are not recorded at all.

**Retention.** For 13 whole months an event keeps its ids. After that a daily job counts the month (events, distinct visitors, pairs of properties compared together), then **clears the ids and keeps the event**, so the event stays as an anonymous record. The policy must not say "deleted after 13 months". Say: linked to a browser for up to 13 months, then made anonymous and kept.

**Who sees it.** Admins, in the admin Analytics screen, which can also open **one browser's event history** (the visitors list and a visitor's journey: what that browser opened, compared and sent, in order, by visit). A browser there is only a random id, with no name, phone or account, and only events that still carry an id (the last 13 months, from browsers that sent no privacy signal) can be opened. Nothing joins a browser to a person, but an admin also has the enquiry inbox, so an admin who matches the time of an enquiry to a journey could infer whose it was; the policy should say admins can see individual browsing history and that access is limited to admins. A developer will see aggregates about their own properties only (never a buyer, never an individual event) once the developer view is built; that view does not exist yet (section 8). No figure is shown to a buyer or becomes a score of a property, and paid presence never affects match order or comparison content.

**Rate limit.** So one caller cannot flood the counts, the events route counts requests per caller address for one minute (120 allowed). The address (as a trusted proxy forwards it) is held **in memory only**, for at most that minute; it is never written to the database or a log, and is not part of an event. `DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-26.

**Consent position taken.** A privacy notice and no consent prompt for analytics, because the events carry no personal identifier; the privacy signal is honoured for the one thing it concerns (a lasting identifier). Revisit if a third-party tracker is ever added. **No claim of "we never store your IP"** may be made without the qualification that the sign-in session stores the IP of a signed-in browser (section 1); the analytics tables hold none.

### 3a. What a developer sees (schema v21, `DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-26)

- **Analytics, aggregates only.** A scheduled job copies figures about a developer's own listed properties into a released table that developer code reads through its own read-only database role. A figure is released only when at least 5 distinct identified visitors are behind it; a figure below that has no number at all (never a zero). It never holds a visitor id, a visit id, a price or a buyer's detail. Fixed windows only (7 days, 30 days, quarter to date, year to date, trailing 12 months). Counts of views and comparisons include events from browsers that sent a privacy signal (they have no visitor id), but the gate counts identified visitors only.
- **Named competitors.** Owner decision: a developer may see which named properties, including another developer's, theirs is most often compared with (not built yet; the pairing must meet the same gate and never carry the rival's own figures).
- **No enquiry figures** in developer analytics: no count, rate or split.
- **A forwarded enquiry, in full.** Once the admin forwards an enquiry, that property's developer sees the buyer's **name, message and phone number**, the property and unit type, and when it was forwarded (`/developers/enquiries`). This is the first time a buyer's phone number reaches a developer. An enquiry not forwarded, or closed, is never shown to a developer. The consent wording at the point of enquiry must say this plainly (section 8.1).

## 4. What is collected about developers and staff

Developer and admin accounts sign in with email and password (Better Auth). Developer invitations, the team panel and the developer organisation's details are held. Staff email addresses and the admin's actions on submissions are held. Brochures and documents a developer or admin uploads, and what is read from them, are property information rather than personal data; they are sent to a third-party reading provider (paid OCR/AI) under `docs/ocr-adapter-usage.md`, which the policy should name generically as a document-reading service. Buyers' data is never sent to that provider.

## 5. Data received from other sources

Property registration details are read from the public GujRERA register (the promoter's and project's details as published). Nothing about a buyer is sent to it.

## 7. Decided, not yet built

- **Scheduling the analytics job** (`bun run analytics:purge`, daily). Until it runs, ids are kept longer than the policy will say.
- **Deleting an account** and what it removes. The schema cascades saves, unlocks and enquiries and clears the link on comparisons and intake answers, but no buyer-facing deletion or export request flow exists.

## 8. Open, and the owner's to decide

1. **Interested-buyer sharing (the product's main data value).** The owner's intent: developers pay for insight and for interested-buyer data, and the policy must carry the consents needed to gather enough of it. Decided so far (2026-09-26): identity is **not** merged into analytics; interested buyers come from what a buyer does under their own account (saves, enquiries) and from an **opt-in consent** captured at sign-in or at an enquiry, stored in its own table. Still open: the exact wording; whether it is one consent or one per purpose (contact by developers of properties they looked at; contact for marketing by us); what a developer sees (name only? phone? a masked number until the buyer replies?); how it is withdrawn; how long it lasts; and whether a buyer can use the site fully without ticking it. **A sentence in the policy is not the consent**: it must be an unticked, purpose-specific choice at the point of collection, recorded with a timestamp and the version of the wording, and it must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
2. **Collecting a name and email** if the business wants them. Today a buyer gives only a phone number, so anything beyond the number has to be asked for (an optional profile step, or on the enquiry form).
3. **Retention** for one-time codes, sign-in sessions (with their IP), intake answers, enquiries, saved items, and closed or deleted accounts. None is defined.
4. **What a developer sees of a forwarded enquiry** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-25 "Enquiry flow").
5. **Buyer age.** Whether buyers under 18 are excluded or handled.
6. **A grievance contact** and a named person to receive rights requests and breach notices.
7. **The landmark map provider** (section 6) and, with it, whether a consent or notice for Google is needed.

## 9. For counsel

- Whether the anonymous-event approach for privacy-signal browsers, and the "linked for 13 months then made anonymous and kept" wording, is adequate under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and its rules (consent, purpose limitation, retention, children, cross-border transfer, breach notice, the rights of a data principal), and under any other law that will apply to buyers abroad.
- Whether a pseudonymous random id counts as personal data for the identified path, and whether a consent prompt is needed for it.
- Whether the opt-in in section 8.1 is drafted correctly, including for enquiries sent to developers.
- Whether keeping the sign-in session's IP address needs its own stated purpose and period.
- The terms of service alongside the policy, and how both are linked from the footer and referenced by the sign-in and enquiry forms.
